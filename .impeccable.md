## Design Context

### Product and audience

NagarSakhi is a phone-first civic participation product for residents of tier-3 Indian municipalities, beginning with a fully synthetic Phusro demo. Many citizens will use lower-end Android phones on intermittent or slow data, and may move naturally between Hindi and English. Ward councillors (Parshads) and corporation administrators use the same product for issue triage, notices, escalations, compliance, and budget visibility, often on larger screens but sometimes on touch devices in the field.

The core jobs are to report and track ward issues, support existing reports rather than create duplicates, understand who is responsible, see notices and public spending, and complete ward tasks. Officials need a calm, auditable workspace for changing issue status, explaining delays, publishing updates, managing escalations, and showing residents what has changed.

### Brand personality

Trustworthy first; local and humane second. The interface should feel like a dependable neighbourhood civic ledger: clear enough for a first-time smartphone user, dignified enough for a public official, and warm enough that reporting a problem does not feel like entering a bureaucratic maze.

The voice is plain, respectful, specific, and bilingual-friendly. It never blames residents, overpromises outcomes, or uses playful language around failures and public safety.

### Aesthetic direction

Use a light, Indian civic-editorial aesthetic rather than a generic SaaS dashboard. The visual memory should be a warm paper-like civic noticeboard crossed with a well-kept municipal register: strong rules, numbered references, prominent ward context, compact evidence rows, and occasional large editorial headings.

Use a warm paper base, deep indigo for trust and primary actions, civic green for verified/completed states, restrained marigold or saffron for attention, and red only for danger or overdue states. These are cultural colour cues, not a literal tricolour treatment. Never use Indian flag motifs, Ashoka Chakra imagery, government seals, official department logos, or styling that implies government endorsement.

Avoid purple/cyan AI gradients, glassmorphism, neon-on-dark styling, oversized metric-card grids, nested cards, generic icon tiles, and patriotic decoration. The product is light-theme only for this release.

### Typography and information design

Typography must remain highly legible for Devanagari and Latin scripts. Prefer one expressive, variable Indian-script-capable family across weights, with tabular numerals for issue numbers, dates, counts, and rupee values. Use a compact five-size hierarchy with strong contrast, never body text below 1rem, and line lengths near 60-70 characters for explanatory copy.

Treat issue numbers, ward names, dates, status labels, and public/private data boundaries as primary information. Use spacing, rules, typography, and alignment for grouping before introducing cards. Data-dense official views may use tables on wider screens, but must transform into labelled, scan-friendly records on small screens.

### Interaction and responsive principles

- Design mobile-first, then add density and side-by-side context as containers widen.
- Keep critical actions available on mobile; adapt rather than hide them.
- Use 44px minimum targets, visible focus rings, visible labels, and keyboard-safe navigation.
- Never make drag-and-drop the only way to change issue status; always offer explicit controls.
- Use optimistic feedback only for reversible, low-stakes actions such as supporting an issue. Confirm server outcomes for status changes, expenditure records, and public notices.
- Prefer inline expansion, drawers, or dedicated panels to modal stacks.
- Motion should clarify state changes using opacity and transform, with reduced-motion support and no bounce effects.
- Assume bilingual copy can expand. Do not encode meaning in colour alone.
- Keep ward and role context persistent so users always know which public area and authority they are acting within.

### Trust and demo safeguards

All visible people, phone numbers, issue media, finances, notices, and municipal records in the demo are synthetic. Show a persistent but unobtrusive demo-data notice. Separate private citizen information from public profiles in both copy and layout. Every consequential official action should expose who changed what and when, and long-running AI or speech work should show explicit queued, processing, succeeded, and failed states rather than holding a web request open.
